Quick Reference

ActualUsing the land in the way an owner of that kind of land would use it — building, farming, fencing, living.Not “present every day” — that is continuity, and it uses the same owner-like measure.
Open and notoriousVisible enough that an owner who checked on the land would notice it. The owner does not have to actually see it.Underground and hidden uses fail here even when everything else is met.
ExclusiveNot shared with the true owner or with the general public.Family and guests are fine; the true owner using it alongside you is fatal.
Hostile (adverse)Without the owner’s permission. In most states, what the possessor believed is irrelevant.Permission means the clock never started. An honest mistake is still hostile.
ContinuousUnbroken for the statutory period, measured by the pattern of use an owner of that land would keep up.Seasonal use of seasonal land counts; a real gap in use does not.
TackingSuccessive possessors add their periods together when possession passes between them — deed, will, inheritance.Needs privity. A stranger who ousts the first possessor starts over.
The periodSet by each state’s limitations statute and counted from the day the owner’s right to sue accrued.Runs against a buyer of the record title too — a sale by the owner does not reset it.
RepudiationWhat a permissive user must do to start the clock: make it plain to the owner that the land is now claimed as the user’s own.Until then, permissive years are worth nothing.
