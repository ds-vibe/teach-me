description: Build a polished, voice-narrated, single-file interactive HTML lesson that teaches a topic to mastery — observable objectives, a taught method, per-concept practice, discrimination drills, typed self-scored answers, and a closed-book final, with spoken rationales and links to primary sources. Use when the user wants to LEARN, PRACTICE, or TEACH something — "teach me X", "make a lesson on X", "help me learn / drill / master X", "build a course module" — rather than read an explainer. # Teach Me

Build lessons that produce **durable learning**: the learner leaves able to DO the thing, not just recognize it. The reader job is "help me learn, retain, and apply" — different from an explainer's "help me understand in one sitting." A quiz makes an explainer stickier; a mastery loop makes it a lesson.

Lineage: forked from `html-explainer` and now **standalone** — the design system, QA battery, and build scripts live in this skill (`reference/design.md`, `reference/qa.md`, `scripts/`), ported and corrected for lessons (bold governs design, never prose). The pedagogy rule spine adapts Rebecca Fordon's MIT-licensed `teaching-explainer` — see `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md`.

**Reference files** (read the ones the phase calls for; they are the source of truth for their area):
`reference/pedagogy.md` — the rule spine + evidence grades · `reference/design.md` — the design system, concept pass, reading shapes, widgets · `reference/media-voice.md` — voice/animation patterns · `reference/usability.md` — lesson chrome, quick reference, source links · `reference/qa.md` — the full QA battery incl. static pre-flight and the prose battery · `reference/js-patterns.md` — proven component implementations.

## Prerequisites (check before promising anything)

- **Standalone**: no other skill is required. The assembler, screenshot loop, and
  drop-in widgets live in this skill's own `scripts/` (`assemble.mjs`, `shoot.mjs`,
  `review-mode.js`, `chat-dock.js`); design and QA rules live in `reference/`.
- Node + Playwright Chromium (the QA loop), plus Playwright WebKit for the mobile pass.
- **Voice (optional):** `OPENAI_API_KEY` (or ElevenLabs) for produced narration, plus ffmpeg — no video involved; `scripts/tts.mjs` uses it to apply the 1.25× speed and compress each line to mono 64 kbps MP3 so the embedded audio fits the size budget. No key → the engine falls back to browser TTS (`speechSynthesis`, labeled "Voice: browser"), and silent captions where even that is missing; the strip test (Phase 5) guarantees the silent tier still teaches. Never block on voice.
- **Companion video (optional):** produced by the separate `video-explainers` skill. **The interview offers the video whether or not that skill is installed** — when it is missing, the offer says plainly that choosing it requires installing `video-explainers` (plus its HyperFrames/ffmpeg prerequisites) before the video phase. Never silently drop the option because the dependency is absent. Video mechanics belong wholly to that skill — teach-me sends it a brief, never edits it.

## Phase 0 — Interview & research

**The eight must-asks:**

1. **Role** — "Are you the educator (building this for others) or the learner (this is for you)?" No default; the answer re-shapes must-asks 2–3:
   - **Learner** → the audience question becomes a **topic-grounded placement probe** — never a bare "beginner / intermediate / advanced." Draft 3–5 concrete landmarks from the topic itself, spanning entry to target, phrased as **do-statements, not recognize-statements** (e.g., for Erie: "state the holding" · "run the twin aims on a fact pattern" · "say why Hanna and Walker come out differently" — recognition self-reports overstate; capability claims don't). Present as select-all-that-apply with a "none of these yet" option, plus the open ask: **where do you get stuck?** Answers are scope input, not just calibration: landmarks the learner has compress to warm-up coverage, shaky ones become the drilled core, and learner-named stuck points join the confusable list with discrimination drills. Nudge grounding toward *their own* course materials (the lesson should match how they are taught and tested; their professor's/instructor's framing governs where it differs from canonical). Personalize the transfer task and honest-limits to their course and deadline.
   - **Educator** → ask the **audience range** with the same topic-grounded landmarks ("can your weakest student do X? your strongest?" — floor and ceiling in concrete performances, which is what the audience-floor test calibrates against), whose terminology/framing governs (syllabus or outline upload near-required), **assessment alignment** (sample real exam/test items as grounding so practice items mirror the actual test format), and how learners will receive the file (delivery constraints; the accessibility and file-size bar rises for a population).
2. **Grounding** — "Do you have your own material to ground in (outline, casebook chapter, syllabus, spec, docs)?" If yes, that material is the **primary source of truth**: it decides scope, framings, and terminology, and the lesson cites it. If no, ground in the canonical public sources for the domain.
3. **Audience & purpose** — who is learning and for what (exam, bar/cert prep, professional refresher, hobby), asked in the role's register per must-ask 1. This calibrates depth, pacing, item difficulty, and which confusables get drilled.
4. **Scope inventory** — enumerate the concepts and variations the lesson will cover, explicitly, at interview time. This list drives the coverage rule (Phase 1): sampling two of ten variations checks attendance, not comprehension. Offer scope bundles (core / comprehensive / core + adjacent layer) when the topic has natural cuts.
5. **Reading shape** — scrolling page (default for lessons: side-by-side comparison, jump-back reference) vs deck (for a genuinely linear sequence; build rules in `reference/design.md`).
6. **Play it straight, or go bold** — never silently default. *Straight*: clean editorial polish, theme as a light accent, one subject-derived element. *Bold*: one committed, cohesive art-direction concept across every surface — judged relevant + cohesive + legible, never on amount (`reference/design.md`). Either way, **prose stays in the plain register — boldness is about visuals, not prose style**.
7. **Voice & media** — narrated walkthroughs + spoken why-feedback (recommended yes when a key exists; state the cost ~1–3 MB and that every spoken line appears on screen). **Default voice: OpenAI `ash` at 1.25× speed** — apply silently; run a voice menu only if the user raises it. Always also offer a **companion video** — if `video-explainers` is not installed, include the heads-up that a yes means installing that skill (a download) before the video gets built; the lesson itself never waits on it. The video: a fully produced 3–5 minute advance organizer built from the same architecture — a high-level gloss of what the page will teach, with 2–3 woven deferral lines naming what the page does deeply ("you'll check your understanding in the final") and an outro CTA to the lesson — for pre-session orientation (educator) or first contact (learner). Content bound to the lesson, treatment at video-explainers' full production strength; never the practice layer. Handoff rules in `reference/media-voice.md`.
8. **AI chat** — BYOK "ask the page" dock. Open yes/no, no default.

**Research gate**: settled topic → build from knowledge. Quotable rule-governed domains (statutes, regulations, standards, APIs) → **fetch the primary text** (2–3 fetches) so every quoted provision is verbatim and verifiable, and every card can link to its source. Dynamic topics → research and date-stamp.

**Silent defaults** (state them; ask only if it matters): single self-contained `.html` file · US/federal framing flagged where jurisdictions differ · session-only state, no accounts · review overlay in · delivery = the file (hosting only on explicit request).

## Phase 1 — Pedagogy architecture (before any design)

Read `reference/pedagogy.md` and apply it. **Instructional design comes first, writing second — answer four questions in this order before drafting any prose:** (1) what misconceptions does this audience likely arrive with? (2) what are the atomic concepts they must acquire, in order? (3) what is the cheapest interaction that lets them *discover* each one? (4) only then write the explanatory prose. Do these moves:

- **Commit before explain.** The lesson opens with one deliberately incomplete "intuition check" and asks the learner to commit. Example: introduce a testimony scenario (witness: "John said the car was blue") and ask "is this hearsay?" *before* explaining the hearsay rule. Here John's statement was out of court (prong one of hearsay), but whether it is offered to prove the matter asserted (prong two) is unanswerable from the prompt — the reveal identifies that missing variable. Each new-enough concept also gets an intuition check with a one-click commit (judgment call: a genuinely new idea earns a commit; a minor variation of the previous concept doesn't) — but make a commit unanswerable only where that serves the pedagogy; usually only the opener is. In each case: (1) the reveal responds to the learner's actual choice, (2) the rule arrives as the answer to a question they now have, (3) the reveal shows the structure the answer turns on — as elements on screen, not just a prose paragraph. This is how the master rationale lands. Reveals are immediate — never withheld. Micro-commits are ungraded and sit outside the item battery (they add clicks, never quiz items).
- **The atom pass.** Every compound definition or rule in the scope inventory gets split before sequencing: list its atoms, mark the ones that do discriminating work, give each working atom its own scenario-commit in which only that atom varies, and state the formal rule LAST — it should read as a recap of parts the learner already owns. History gets the same treatment as a **causal chain** (actor → motive → consequence → new incentive), never a dated timeline, with at least one link — usually the exploitation step — as a learner prediction. Details: pedagogy.md rules 14–15.
- **Why it exists comes first.** Open with the problem the thing solves, felt concretely, before the definition and before the method. Method without purpose doesn't transfer. Where an individual concept has a real rationale of its own, its card states it, tied to the master rationale — but in the domain's own idiom (a rule's purpose, a chord's function, a query's use case), and only where it genuinely illuminates. A literal "WHY THIS EXISTS" block on every card is one build's framing (the hearsay lesson), not universal anatomy — don't add that wording to every topic.
- **Objectives**: 3–6 observable "after this, the learner can…" statements. Everything maps to one; anything mapping to none is cut.
- **Organizing structure**: confirm with the user (process framework / conceptual model / plain sequence — never invent one the material doesn't have).
- **The pedagogy concept pass**: name each objective's **target performance** (what the learner actually DOES — play the moves, classify the statement, run the query), then generate **2–3 distinct mechanics concepts** for teaching it. The primary interaction must practice the target performance in its own medium: bespoke widgets for procedural/spatial skills; classify/MC-style items when classification IS the skill. Verbal matching about a procedural skill is the classic failure. **The wrongness test**: an interaction is constructive only if the learner's input can be wrong (grades: commit → classify → produce → perform); watch-only sequences are illustrative — legitimate support, zero coverage credit, and never the centerpiece. Bucket-sort (click-to-assign a stimulus to one of N categories) is a strong centerpiece form when the skill is purpose- or category-judgment. **Audience-floor test:** an interaction the stated audience answers without thinking teaches nothing — fold it into exposition. **The screen-answerable test (harder form of the floor):** for every drill, name what the learner must recall or produce *that is not visible on screen at answer time*. A drill answerable by reading the current screen is a reading task, not a check (shipped once: "click the loci in order" on a plan whose loci were numbered on screen). **Never drill the scaffolding**: a fixture the lesson invented (a training board, a sample palace) exists to make the real skill checkable — memorizing the fixture itself is not an objective and gets no drills.
- **Coverage plan** from the scope inventory: every item gets teaching + ≥1 retrieval check; **confusable pairs get discrimination checks** (which one is this, and why not the neighbor); **boundary rules get a mutation pair** — after a correct answer, the same scenario returns with one fact changed and the learner re-rules ("same image, but you put it at the sink instead of where the route expects it — what breaks?"); same-facts-one-change maps a rule's edge more precisely than a fresh scenario; one **typed self-scored item per section** (type → model answer → self-score against a **named-criteria checklist**, not a global verdict: 2–4 per-item criteria the learner verifies mechanically in their own text — "your image has a verb", "the locus appears in your scene"; rule-application items always include the **because-test**: "your answer has a sentence linking a specific fact to a specific element — find your 'because'"); a **mixed closed-book final** at the end (support panels locked until every item is *answered* — release on attempt, not perfection; **role-framed where the domain has a performer role** — "you're the judge: ten rulings, one objection each" — same battery, real stakes). **Battery size defaults substantial — roughly 2–3 items per inventory concept** — scaled down only for small topics, up for exam prep. **The 2–3 is a coverage floor against skipping concepts, not a quota**: a low-difficulty judgment concept (e.g. "pick a familiar location") gets ONE good discrimination item, and the freed weight goes to the checkable performance work. Four multiple-choice items about the same easy call is bloat, not rigor.
- **Multi-modal reinforcement** (spine rule 12): the master rationale (where the material has one), the method, and each concept's core idea land as text + visual + voice-where-available; the organizing model appears in multiple visual forms (list, animated diagram, rebuilt-by-learner). Narration complements on-screen text, never reads it verbatim.
- **Source anchoring** (spine rule 13): teach from the primary text — verbatim quotes on cards, links to the canonical source, the quick-reference shelf as the real provisions in miniature. Reading the actual authority is part of the target skill; user-supplied grounding material anchors the same way.
- **Staged practice**: interleaved with teaching, section by section — never massed at the end. Blocked sets before mixed sets. Worked example → learner replay/attempt (hints opt-in, one step at a time) → cold application.
- If a **method/algorithm** organizes the topic: teach it as a list AND an animated decision diagram, traced on a worked example, restated late, and rebuilt by the learner (ordering exercise). Every branch of the diagram is a legal/logical claim — it gets verified (Phase 5).

## Phase 2 — Decision checkpoint (MANDATORY — user chooses before you build)

Do not build past this point on assumptions. Present one package:

- **2–3 rendered style frames** — throwaway HTML, screenshotted, real content, never text labels (a frame shows the actual palette, type, and the signature device). Both straight and bold builds get frames.
- **The 2–3 pedagogy-mechanics concepts** from Phase 1, stated as what the learner will do, with a recommendation.
- **Voice & media plan** — where narration and spoken feedback go; what (if anything) earns animation.
- **Assumed defaults the interview didn't cover** — point of view, orientation, register of address — anything a user might veto.

The user picks. A spec or scope approval does NOT cover these choices. Record picks and any vetoes in the project's planning file; every later QA pass checks against the vetoes.

## Phase 3 — Design system

`reference/design.md` is the source of truth (the quality bar, tokens, banned looks, contrast, spacing, the concept pass). Lesson-specific chrome on top (details in `reference/usability.md`):

- **Lesson chrome is telegraphic**: headers, labels, buttons are plain nouns ("Section 803 Exceptions", "Quiz and Practice", "Your Turn"). Written phrasing lives in prose and narration only.
- **Unified concept-card anatomy**, one shape repeated for every concept/rule/exception — and the card is the *resolution*, not the opener:
  **scenario + one-click commit above the card** (for every concept with a real rationale; the reveal responds to the learner's choice) → then the card: name + one-line gist → rationale where the concept earns one (labeled in the domain's idiom — "Purpose", "What it's for", "The sound", or a build-specific label; + Listen button when voiced) → **the source text, verbatim** (long provisions abridged with remainders quoted in full behind expanders) + **link out to the source** → worked example → quick check. Definition-first section order is the failure this replaces: the rule text lands as the answer to the commit, never as the introduction. Uniformity of *shape* across cards is the rule; the rationale slot's label and presence adapt to the topic.
- **Quick Reference shelf** (default ON for legal/technical/rule-based topics): compact per-rule summaries in a side panel, opened from a plainly-labeled button in the persistent top bar, sticky header + close, full-text source links at the bottom. Lockable during closed-book work.
- **Guided flow**: "Next · [section] ↓" link at every section end. Readers never wonder where to go.
- **Diagrams sit beside their text**, not above; footnotes run full width; cut any list that restates the diagram. **Structural visuals**: where a section's material has structure (a method, a chronology, a paired contrast), render it as a diagram — decision tree, timeline rail, comparison panel; an all-text section prompts that check, never forced decoration (`reference/usability.md`).
- Progress: objective chips in the top bar + a mastery checklist section at the end. A final honest-limits block: what the lesson does NOT teach, jurisdiction/version caveats, and a transfer task ("this week, apply the method to one real X").

## Phase 4 — Build

- **Prose register — FIRST, before writing a single sentence: draft in plain English with no AI slop.** This isn't post-hoc QA, it's a first-draft rule; the QA register audit is a net for anything that slips through, but slop caught there costs time to remove. Read this rule BEFORE drafting and hew to it, everywhere (page copy AND narration `LINES`): plain expository teaching prose, subject-verb-object, concrete nouns and examples, ordinary verbs, one idea per sentence; explain, don't perform. North-star sample: "A memory palace helps you remember lengthy lists by visualizing them in places you already know, like the rooms of your childhood home. These locations are called loci…"
  **The five slop classes, by contrast (examples are the rule):**
  - *Verb inflation* — "Interrogate the ownership structure." → "Compare who owns what before and after financing."
  - *Oblique abstraction* — "Stakeholders have competing priorities." → "Employees want upside. Investors want to limit dilution. Founders want control."
  - *False compression* — "Price the grant." → "Estimate what the grant could be worth under different exit scenarios."
  - *False sophistication* — "Navigate the landscape of equity compensation." → "Learn how RSUs and stock options work."
  - *Fake insight* (the most pernicious: satisfying to read, adds nothing) — "Equity is ultimately about aligning incentives." → "Startups often pay partly in equity because they cannot match big-company salaries."
  Ordinary verbs win: identify, compare, calculate, explain, ask, estimate, list, choose, determine, show — never navigate, unlock, interrogate, illuminate, leverage, surface, crystallize, orchestrate, operationalize, contextualize.
  **Banned register classes** (all shipped in real builds and passed the named-shapes slop battery): slightly-off figurative verbs ("hold"/"keep" where the plain word is "remember"), forced framing nouns ("the method is a *trade*"), incantation phrasing ("walked one way, *forever*", "no walk ever starts anywhere else"), flourish-verbs ("hands the order back *for free*", "watch them land"), load-bearing aphorisms ("The gap between the two scores is the lesson"), summarizing pronouncements ("That is the whole skill:", "That is the whole trade"), and figurative verbs on mechanical/mathematical operations ("the full week *vanishes*" — say "you can ignore full weeks; only the remainder moves the weekday").
  **Final test, per sentence:** who is acting? what are they doing? why? can every abstract noun become a concrete one? would a domain expert say this aloud? If the plain version is shorter, clearer, and equally accurate, it ships. A couple of earned images per lesson, maximum — communicating, not showing off.
- **Hybrid build path**: write the raw file, then run
  `<this-skill>/scripts/assemble.mjs raw.html -o lesson.html`
  (injects the review overlay from the local `scripts/review-mode.js`, validates IDs;
  add `--chat` when the user opted into the AI dock). The scripts are part of this
  skill — resolve the skill's own root from the active runtime; no external skill path.
- **Components**: adapt the proven implementations in `reference/js-patterns.md` — voice engine (per-line audio, single on-screen transcript channel), ruling/MC widget (words-not-color verdicts, why-feedback, retry, reteach routing), typed self-score item, ordering chips, animated decision diagram, quick-reference drawer with lock, mastery ledger. Plain ES5-style JS, no dependencies, real apostrophes in JS strings (entities in `textContent` render literally — a recurring bug).
- **Voice** (`reference/media-voice.md` + `scripts/tts.mjs`): one `LINES` map is the single source for text and audio — TTS generates FROM the page so they can never drift; per-line files, sequential playback gates animation steps; every spoken line visible on screen (caption during narration, verdict for feedback — never both); audio buttons carry a speaker icon and a color distinct from CTAs; reserve fixed heights for text that changes during playback; nothing plays sound without a click; global voice toggle. **Degrade ladder: embedded MP3 → browser TTS → silent** — build the fallback tier into the engine (js-patterns.md has the pattern; toggle labeled "Voice: browser"). Whisper transcript-verify sampled takes.
- **Self-containment**: `node scripts/fonts.mjs lesson.raw.html` inlines web fonts as data URIs — offline-proof, host-proof. Audio embeds as base64 (~30 lines ≈ 1.3 MB; budget ~4 MB total).
- Content in structured data (item arrays), separate from presentation. Keep every fact pattern's designated answer defensible — distractors are the *neighboring* concepts, not junk.

## Phase 5 — QA (required; never skip; details in `reference/qa.md`)

Run the full battery in `reference/qa.md` — Step-0 static pre-flight, the screenshot-and-look loop (`scripts/shoot.mjs`), and the prose battery — PLUS the lesson gates:

- **Pedagogy gates**: every objective taught + checked · every scope-inventory item covered · discrimination checks present for confusables · why-feedback + retry + reteach routing on every wrong answer · **strip test** (remove all voice/media — every objective must still be taught and checkable) · access locks release on attempt.
- **State walk**: a Playwright script (start from `scripts/qa-states.template.mjs`) that exercises **every interactive path including the wrong answers**, asserting zero page errors, plus a WebKit iPhone pass asserting zero horizontal overflow.
- **Full-page render re-read after ANY structural HTML edit** — a zero-error state walk does not verify layout; one stray closing tag silently collapses a grid.
- **The unbiased reviewer** (non-negotiable): a fresh-context agent that did not write the draft, briefed with **the rules and the user's veto ledger only — never the writer's justifications or exemptions**. A writer-authored exemption in the brief defeats the pass. The reviewer checks: legal/domain accuracy of every item's designated answer and every "why" (blockers for any error) · verbatim-quotation claims against the fetched primary text · **domain-legality of every scripted animation and diagram branch** (a scripted demo is a claim) · the slop battery · framing claims ("a common belief…" asserts sociology; prefer "at first glance…").
- Fix, re-run affected passes, and re-read the render. Done = all passes clear, not "it builds."

## Phase 6 — Deliver & revise

- Deliverable = the single `.html` file (+ the *Review & edit* link from the overlay). **Never deploy or publish anywhere without the user's explicit instruction** — a problem statement ("we need mobile to work") is not authorization. When mobile matters, propose hosting options with the command ready and wait.
- Expect an optics/usability round on the real artifact — collect it as numbered items, fix all, re-run QA. Record user vetoes immediately; they bind all future revisions.
- **After inserting or moving a beat, re-experience the flow around it in every channel.** Read (and listen) from one beat before to one beat after: narration lines, captions, static ledes, and the next section's intro were all written against the old sequence, and any of them can silently still assume it (a narration that opens cold as if the learner had not just committed is the proven failure).
- Keep a per-project planning file: choices, vetoes, scope inventory, QA receipts.

## Anti-patterns

Verbal exercises standing in for the target skill · practice massed at the end · **definition-first section order** (the rule text before the scenario-commit it should answer) · **a watch-only centerpiece** · **history as a dated timeline** (the causal chain is the content) · **counting micro-commits toward battery sizing** (or grading them) · checks that sample a fraction of the enumerated scope · **drills answerable by reading the current screen** (a numbered-on-screen "click in order" is a reading task) · **drilling the lesson's own scaffolding** (memorizing the invented fixture) · **item quotas over item value** (four MCs on one easy judgment call) · all-hints-at-once scaffolding · perfect-score access locks · narration printed as static text · "Play" as a bare text button · metaphor systems dressing headers and labels · **performed prose in teaching copy or narration** (figurative-verb drift, incantations, load-bearing aphorisms — see the Phase 4 register rule) · paraphrase styled as verbatim rule text · a diagram with a wrong arrow (the worst bug a lesson can ship) · overclaiming learning-science evidence (no direct outcome research exists on interactive HTML lessons; say so if asked) · deploying without being asked.

## Definition of done

Every qa.md Step-0 pre-flight box passes · Phase 5 lesson battery clear, including the unbiased review with zero unresolved blockers · every scope item taught + checked · sources linked; quotes verbatim or marked · voice (if on) transcript-verified, toggleable, click-gated · state walk + mobile pass green · honest-limits block present · the file delivered.
