# The pedagogy spine

The learning-science layer. Rules tagged **[F]** are adapted from Rebecca Fordon's `teaching-explainer` (MIT — see THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md), **[F+]** adapted and extended, **[new]** original to this skill, proven on real builds (a chess opening lesson; a hearsay/evidence lesson) but not yet in the graded evidence base.

## Evidence base — read before adding or strengthening any rule

Claims stay at the strength of their evidence (grades adopted from Fordon's review):

- **There is no direct outcome research on interactive single-page HTML lessons.** The case is argued by analogy from well-studied cousins. Say so when asked; never claim the format itself has proven learning gains.
- **Strong:** retrieval practice (g ≈ 0.50–0.70; benefits held only when material was retrieved at least twice — the floor behind rule 4) · active learning over passive lecture (Freeman et al. 2014, +0.47 SD) · the coherence principle — cutting decoration — is the largest measured multimedia effect (g ≈ 1.00, Cromley & Chen 2025).
- **Qualified:** multimedia principles average only g ≈ 0.37 and are not uniformly effective · ICAP (interactive > constructive > active > passive) is strong as a framework, contested on strict ordering · elaborative interrogation ("why is this true?") grades moderate-utility — real, not magic.
- **Contested:** UDL's measured effectiveness — use it to structure representation choices, never to claim gains.
- **Debunked:** learning-styles matching (Pashler et al. 2008). Hard guardrail, rule 10.

## The rule spine

1. **Observable objectives first** [new] — 3–6 "after this, the learner can…" statements drive everything. Every section, demo, and check maps to one; anything mapping to none is cut.
2. **User-confirmed organizing structure** [F] — a process framework, a conceptual model, or a plain logical sequence (the fallback, never the default). Never impose a structure the user doesn't recognize as their own; if no honest structure emerges, don't invent one.
3. **≥1 constructive interaction per key concept, aimed at the target performance** [F+] — predict, classify, self-explain, or generate. Click-to-reveal alone is disclosure, not construction. **The wrongness test defines constructive**: an interaction is constructive if and only if the learner's input can be wrong. A slider, a Watch button, a click-through diagram — no possible wrongness, information flows page→learner only; that is *illustrative*, legitimate for showing mechanisms but worth zero coverage credit and never the centerpiece. Constructive interactions come in grades, cheapest first: **commit** (one-click prediction, ungraded) · **classify** (pick the category, graded, why-feedback) · **produce** (typed, criteria-scored) · **perform** (multi-step practicum). **Cadence**: aim for one learner decision per concept-chunk of teaching; micro-commits are ungraded and sit OUTSIDE the item battery and its sizing rules — this rule adds clicks, never quiz items. **The interaction must practice what the objective says the learner can do**: procedural/spatial skills get bespoke widgets that perform the skill in its own medium; classification/analysis skills get classify- and ruling-style items where the wrong options are the neighboring concepts. Verbal exercises *about* a procedural skill are the classic failure (sentence-matching where move practice belonged). **The screen-answerable test**: every check must require the learner to recall or produce something *not visible on screen at answer time* — an item answerable by reading the current view is a reading task, not practice (proven failure: a click-the-stops-in-order drill on a plan whose stops were numbered on screen). And **never drill the scaffolding**: invented fixtures (a training board, a sample palace) exist to make the real skill checkable; memorizing the fixture is not an objective.
4. **Retrieval coverage scales with the concept inventory — ≥2 checks is the floor, not the target** [F+] — enumerate concepts and variations at architecture time; each gets at least one retrieval touch, distributed (never stacked at the end); the lesson closes with a mixed cumulative round. **Known confusable pairs get discrimination checks** (which one is this, and why not the other). **Rules with a boundary worth learning get a mutation pair** [new — adapted from law-school hypo drilling]: after a correct answer, the same scenario returns with exactly one fact changed and the learner rules again — the pair teaches where the rule flips, which fresh unrelated scenarios never isolate. Every check carries why-feedback (the correct reasoning, not praise or the bare answer), a retry path, and a reteach pointer back to the section that covers it. Low stakes; no stored grades.
   **Sizing the battery**: the default is substantial — roughly **2–3 items per inventory concept**, structured as per-section practice (a couple of applied items + one typed) → blocked sets by concept family → a mixed closed-book final of 6–10 items. A rule system with ten variations wants ~20–25 items total (the hearsay build: 19 applied + 5 typed). Scale DOWN only for genuinely small topics; scale UP for exam-prep audiences. Sampling a fraction of the inventory checks attendance, not comprehension. **But the number is a floor against skipping, not a quota**: an easy judgment concept gets one good discrimination item, and the freed weight moves to the performance work — repeated MCs on the same low-difficulty call read as padding and cost the learner's trust.
5. **Worked example → faded attempt → independent application** [new] — for skill-shaped objectives. The worked example may be a narrated animation (hands-off); the faded and independent steps must be live and constructive. **Fading is learner-controlled**: hints are opt-in and reveal one step at a time, default off. **Practice runs to the tension**: a sequence drill reaches the payoff or decision point, not the minimal skeleton. **Typed items self-score against named criteria, never a global verdict** [new — adapted from IRAC grading]: 2–4 per-item criteria the learner can verify mechanically in their own text ("your sentence has a verb"; "the locus appears in your scene"), shown at reveal in place of a lone Got-it/Partly/Missed-it row. Rule-application items always include the **because-test**: the answer must contain a sentence linking a specific fact to a specific element — restating rule and facts side by side is the classic failure, and "find your 'because'" is the mechanical check for it.
6. **Coherence — cut decoration** [F] — every visual element labels a concept, shows a relationship, or illustrates a process. The best-evidenced rule here, and the anti-slop battery restated in learning-science terms.
7. **Misconception diagnosis → targeted remediation** [new] — where classic wrong beliefs exist, checks are written so wrong answers reveal *which* misconception, and route to the specific section that reteaches it.
8. **Transfer task** [new] — at least one application in a context the lesson didn't teach in, near the end ("this week, run the method on one real X").
9. **Honest mastery criteria and explicit limits** [F+] — per-objective checks gate the "done" state; the lesson states what it does NOT teach, what real practice requires, and version/jurisdiction caveats.
10. **No learning-styles adaptation** [F] — never detect, infer, or adapt to styles. Instead: every substantive concept in multiple representations (prose + visual + interaction + optional voice), for all learners.
11. **Teach why it exists before how it works** [new] — open with the problem the thing solves, felt concretely, before the definition and the method. The rationale is the organizing schema; where a sub-rule or concept has a real rationale of its own, its card states it against the master rationale — that is what makes a rule system cohere instead of reading as a list. **The framing is the domain's, not a template's**: a legal rule gets its purpose, a chord gets its sound function, a query pattern gets its use case. The literal "WHY THIS EXISTS" card label came from the hearsay build and is not universal anatomy — pick a label (or none) that fits the topic, and skip the slot entirely on concepts whose only honest rationale is a restatement of the master one.
   **Fractal, and delivered as a commit — not prose-once-at-the-top.** The rule applies at the lesson opening AND at each concept different enough from what preceded it — a judgment call, not a per-card stamp (a genuinely new idea earns a commit; a minor variation of the previous concept doesn't). The commit is a concrete scenario and one click *before* the teaching; the reveal shows the structure the answer turns on as on-screen elements (atoms, chain links), not only prose about it; the reveal responds to the learner's actual choice and names what their current model was missing; the rule text then arrives as the answer to a question they now have. Reveals are immediate — commits are hooks, never withheld answers (pretesting/generation effects are moderate and real; unguided discovery reliably fails for novices — this is the cheap guided form). **One reframing opener per lesson**: the lesson's opening commit is deliberately unanswerable with the naive model ("Hearsay? — Not enough information: it depends on why the lawyer offered it") — that is the standard way the master rationale lands.
12. **Key content lands in at least two modalities** [new — beyond Fordon] — the load-bearing pieces (the master rationale where the material has one, the method, each concept's core idea) reach the learner as text + visual, and as voice where available: narrated walkthroughs, spoken rationales on cards, spoken why-feedback. The organizing model appears in **multiple visual forms** (list, animated diagram, learner-rebuilt exercise, progress rail) — different looks at one model, not decoration. Evidence: multimedia principle g ≈ 0.68 (words + pictures over words alone, Cromley & Chen 2025) — one of the stronger effects; honest caveats: narration must complement on-screen text, never read it verbatim (redundancy principle), and voice is reinforcement, not a requirement (the strip test still gates).
13. **Anchor in the source documents** [new — beyond Fordon] — teach from the primary text, not about it: each concept card quotes the governing source **verbatim** (abridgments marked, remainders in full) and **links to the canonical text**; the quick-reference shelf is the real provisions in miniature; practice items send wrong answers back to the source. In rule-governed domains, reading the actual authority IS part of the target skill — summaries train dependence on summaries, and the learner who has only met paraphrases transfers nothing to the real document. When the user supplies grounding material (outline, casebook, spec), that material is the anchor and the lesson cites it the same way.

14. **The atom pass — split compound rules before teaching them** [new] — a compound definition ("an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted") is several moving parts, and teaching it whole makes the learner parse all of them at once. Before sequencing: list the atoms, mark which do real discriminating work (for hearsay: *offered for what purpose* and *truth of the matter asserted* — not "out-of-court"), give each working atom its own scenario-and-commit in which **only that atom varies**, and state the formal rule LAST, where it reads as a recap of parts the learner already owns. Trivial atoms are taught in passing, no ceremony. Mutation pairs (rule 4) are the same instrument after teaching: atoms build the rule going in, mutations test its boundaries coming out.

15. **History is a causal chain, never a dated timeline** [new] — for doctrine and design-decision topics, each historical step is a motivated actor responding to the incentive the previous step created: reasonable idea → the incentive it set up → the exploitation → the scandal or failure → the fix. Dates decorate; the chain teaches, because the chain IS the rule's rationale — a learner who has the chain can reconstruct the rule. **At least one link is a learner prediction**, usually the exploitation step, because incentives are predictable ("you run the taxicab company; the federal rule favors you and state law doesn't — what do you do?"): the learner invents the abuse, then meets the case that did it.

**Build order:** objectives (1) → structure (2) → atom pass (14) → pedagogy concept pass (below) → sequencing → assign interactions, checks, and example ladders (3, 4, 5, 7, 11-commits) → build → gates. Rules 6, 10, and 12 are standing constraints; 8 and 9 close the lesson.

**Access gates release on attempt, not perfection** — a closed-book mode may lock supports while active, but the lock releases when every item is *answered*; perfect-score gating can lock a struggling learner out permanently. Only checklist credit requires correctness.

**Lesson chrome is telegraphic** — plain-noun headers, labels, buttons; written phrasing lives in prose and narration. Learners scan chrome; they read paragraphs.

**Media in the spine** — passive media never satisfies rule 3. An animation may only BE the worked example (rule 5), and voice may only CARRY feedback (rules 4/7) or serve access. A clip doing any other job is decoration; rule 6 cuts it. **Strip test is a gate**: with every clip and voice element removed, each objective must still be taught and checkable.

## The pedagogy concept pass

Interaction design derives from what the topic actually is, decided before architecture:

1. Name each objective's **target performance** — the thing the learner will actually do.
2. Generate **2–3 distinct mechanics concepts** for teaching it (e.g., narrated demo → replay-from-memory → cold checks · a ruling loop with neighboring-rule distractors · a drill ladder with fading hints · an annotated worked case the learner takes over). Each specifies the primary interaction per objective and where explanation, voice, and media sit.
3. Apply rule 3's constraint; run the audience-floor test (an interaction the stated audience answers without thinking gets folded into exposition).
4. **Surface the concepts at the decision checkpoint** with a recommendation — the user chooses the mechanics the same way they choose the visual direction. Don't silently pick when the choice changes what the learner spends time doing.

## Modes

- **Async** (default): immediate why-feedback, fully self-contained, offline-capable.
- **In-class**: reveals withheld for live discussion, projection-friendly type, synchronous checks.
- The line against an LMS holds in both: single artifact, single topic, no accounts, ever.
